TAXONOMY REFERENCE: TANGIBILITY AXIS

CATEGORY 1: MATERIAL
Definition: All more-than-human living entities, non-living natural elements, ecosystems, and their physical derivatives that possess physical presence, occupy space, have mass, and are directly captured by the camera in the real world.
Inclusion Scope:
- Real-World Organisms and Ecosystems: The physical presence of more-than-human living entities (flora, fauna, fungi, and microorganisms) and the natural or semi-natural environments where they interact when directly captured by the camera (for example, trees, flowers, insects, mammals, mushrooms, bacteria, forests, coral reefs, and beaches).
- Physical Non-Living Elements: Observable hydrological, atmospheric, meteorological, geological, topographical, and astrophysical elements directly captured by the camera in reality (for example, rivers, oceans, rain, snow, wind, mountains, sand, rocks, mineral oil, and the sun).
- Substances, Derivatives and Artefacts: Edible solid or liquid items at any processing level, and manufactured objects where the natural material of origin remains visually identifiable, when directly captured by the camera in the real world (for example, salad, coffee, meat, butter, pasta, canned tuna, wool, leather, wooden tables with visible wood grain, and houses made of stone).

CATEGORY 2: IMMATERIAL
Definition: Stylized, abstract, artistic, scientific, or fictional depictions that represent natural elements, processes, or ecosystems. This explicitly includes indirect captures where the camera takes a picture of a representation of nature rather than the physical natural entity itself (such as a picture of a picture).
Inclusion Scope:
- Indirect and Framed Depictions: A camera capturing a representation of nature displayed on, or printed within, another medium or surface (for example, a picture of a tablet in which a picture of a dog is displayed, a picture of a book cover that contains mountains, or a picture of an advertising panel with a beach on it).
- Fictional Representations: Depictions that reimagine or fabricate nature in non-literal forms (for example, video game scenes containing forests, mountain ranges or coral reefs, and cartoon animals and plants).
- Artistic and Abstract Representations: Creative, expressive, stylized, or abstract interpretations of nature (for example, paintings of flora and fauna, and flowers printed on dresses).
- Physical Representational Objects: Three-dimensional manufactured objects explicitly designed to depict fauna and flora rather than being the natural entities themselves (for example, stuffed animals, stuffed plants, and figurines or statues of fauna and flora).
