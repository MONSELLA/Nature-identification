TAXONOMY REFERENCE: LIFE CATEGORY AXIS

CATEGORY 1: BIOTIC
Definition: All more-than-human living organisms, ecosystems defined by living communities, and the material or immaterial results and derivatives of biological processes.
Inclusion Scope:
- Flora & Plant Derivatives: Wild or potted plants, flowers, trees, and plant-derived foods, drinks, or materials (e.g., sugar, coffee, liqueur, wood with visible grain).
- Fauna & Animal Derivatives: Wild, domesticated, or bred non-human vertebrates, molluscs, and insects, as well as animal-derived products (e.g., butter, honey, meat, eggs).
- Fungi & Microorganisms: Wild or cultivated mushrooms, moulds, yeasts, and bacteria.
- Biological Ecosystems: Environments defined by the living communities and biological interactions within them (e.g., nature reserves, urban parks, vegetable gardens, forests).
- Representations: Fictional, scientific, or artistic depictions of non-human living organisms or biological products (e.g., animated animals, drawings of plants).

CATEGORY 2: ABIOTIC
Definition: All non-living physical, chemical, geological, topographical, meteorological, or astrophysical elements and processes that characterize or occur within an ecosystem.
Inclusion Scope:
- Hydrological Components: Water bodies and fluid flows (e.g., rivers, ponds, lakes, oceans, streams).
- Atmospheric & Weather Components: Meteorological phenomena and seasonal climatic conditions (e.g., rain, clouds, snow, wind, ambient temperature, winter snowstorms).
- Geological & Topographical Components: Large-scale landforms, earth structures, natural mineral resources, and soil materials (e.g., mountains, canyons, cliffs, valleys, caves, dunes, rocks, sand, salt, mineral oil).
- Astrophysical Components & Processes: Celestial bodies and phenomena (e.g., the sun, stars, moon, sunsets, sunrises, eclipses, natural day/night lighting).
- Representations: Fictional, scientific, or artistic depictions of non-living natural elements, landforms, or phenomena (e.g., paintings of a mountain range or sunset, video-game weather effects).
